# DPDP Act 2023 — Chapter & Section Index

The Digital Personal Data Protection Act, 2023 (No. 22 of 2023). Assented 11 August 2023. 9 chapters, 44 sections, 1 Schedule. Sections come into force on dates the Central Government notifies (§1(2)); different dates may apply to different provisions.

## Scope (§3)
- Applies to processing of digital personal data **within India** where collected in digital form, or in non-digital form and **digitised subsequently**.
- Applies **extraterritorially** to processing **outside India** if connected with offering goods/services to Data Principals **within India**.
- Does **not** apply to: personal/domestic processing by an individual; or personal data made publicly available by the Data Principal herself or by anyone under a legal obligation to publish it.

## Chapter I — Preliminary (§§1–3)

| § | Short title | Essence |
|---|---|---|
| 1 | Short title and commencement | Names the Act; commences on date(s) notified by Central Government, possibly staggered by provision. |
| 2 | Definitions | Defines key terms (see verbatim block below). |

## Chapter II — Obligations of Data Fiduciary (§§4–10)

| § | Short title | Essence |
|---|---|---|
| 4 | Grounds for processing personal data | Process only for a lawful purpose, on the basis of (a) consent or (b) certain legitimate uses. |
| 5 | Notice | Consent request must be accompanied/preceded by a notice stating data, purpose, how to exercise rights, and how to complain to the Board. |
| 6 | Consent | Consent must be free, specific, informed, unconditional, unambiguous, with clear affirmative action; itemised; withdrawable as easily as given. |
| 7 | Certain legitimate uses | Closed list of non-consent processing grounds (voluntary provision, State functions/subsidies, legal compliance, medical emergency, epidemics, disasters, employment). |
| 8 | General obligations of Data Fiduciary | Accountability, processor contracts, accuracy/completeness, security safeguards, breach intimation, erasure/retention limits, DPO contact, grievance redressal. |
| 9 | Processing of personal data of children | Verifiable parental/guardian consent; no detrimental processing; no tracking, behavioural monitoring, or targeted ads to children. |
| 10 | Additional obligations of Significant Data Fiduciary | SDF must appoint India-based DPO, independent data auditor, and run periodic DPIA + audit. |

## Chapter III — Rights and Duties of Data Principal (§§11–15)

| § | Short title | Essence |
|---|---|---|
| 11 | Right to access information about personal data | Obtain summary of data processed, processing activities, and identities of fiduciaries/processors it was shared with. |
| 12 | Right to correction and erasure of personal data | Correct, complete, update, and erase personal data (subject to legal retention). |
| 13 | Right of grievance redressal | Readily available redressal from fiduciary/Consent Manager; response within prescribed period; exhaust before approaching Board. |
| 14 | Right to nominate | Nominate an individual to exercise rights on death/incapacity. |
| 15 | Duties of Data Principal | Comply with law, no impersonation, no suppression of material info, no false/frivolous grievances, furnish authentic info. |

## Chapter IV — Special Provisions (§§16–17)

| § | Short title | Essence |
|---|---|---|
| 16 | Processing of personal data outside India | Central Government may, by notification, **restrict transfer to notified countries/territories** (blacklist model); higher-protection laws are preserved. |
| 17 | Exemptions | Carve-outs for legal claims, courts/regulators, offence prevention, foreign-contract processing of non-India principals, M&A, default/financial-info; State-instrumentality and research/archiving/statistics exemptions; startup relief; 5-year power to exempt classes. |

## Chapter VI — Powers, Functions and Procedure to be Followed by Board (§§27–28)

| § | Short title | Essence |
|---|---|---|
| 27 | Powers and functions of Board | Inquire into breaches (data breach, fiduciary/Consent Manager obligations, intermediary §37(2)) and impose penalties; issue binding directions. |
| 28 | Procedure to be followed by Board | Functions as independent digital-by-design office; inquiry process; civil-court powers (summons, evidence, inspection); may penalise false/frivolous complaints. |

## Chapter VII — Appeal and Alternate Dispute Resolution (§§29–32)

| § | Short title | Essence |
|---|---|---|
| 29 | Appeal to Appellate Tribunal | Appeal to TDSAT within **60 days**; Tribunal endeavours to dispose within **six months**; digital-by-design. |
| 30 | Orders passed by Appellate Tribunal to be executable as decree | Tribunal orders enforceable as civil-court decrees. |
| 31 | Alternate dispute resolution | Board may refer a complaint to mediation. |
| 32 | Voluntary undertaking | Board may accept a voluntary undertaking; acceptance bars proceedings unless the undertaking is breached. |

## Chapter VIII — Penalties and Adjudication (§§33–34)

| § | Short title | Essence |
|---|---|---|
| 33 | Penalties | On finding a **significant** breach, Board imposes monetary penalty per the Schedule, weighing nature/gravity/duration, repetition, gain/loss, mitigation, proportionality, impact. |
| 34 | Crediting sums realised by way of penalties to Consolidated Fund of India | All penalties credited to the Consolidated Fund of India. |

## Chapter IX — Miscellaneous (§§35–44)

| § | Short title | Essence |
|---|---|---|
| 35 | Protection of action taken in good faith | No suit/prosecution against Govt/Board/Members for good-faith acts. |
| 36 | Power to call for information | Central Government may require Board/fiduciary/intermediary to furnish information. |
| 37 | Power of Central Government to issue directions | After ≥2 penalties on a fiduciary, Govt may direct blocking of public access to its computer resource; intermediaries must comply. |
| 38 | Consistency with other laws | Act is in addition to other laws; prevails to the extent of conflict. |
| 39 | Bar of jurisdiction | Civil courts barred from matters the Board is empowered over; no injunctions. |
| 40 | Power to make rules | Central Government rule-making power over the enumerated matters. |
| 41 | Laying of rules and certain notifications | Rules/§16 & §42 notifications laid before Parliament for 30 days; subject to modification/annulment. |
| 42 | Power to amend Schedule | Govt may amend the Schedule, but cannot increase any penalty to **more than twice** the originally enacted amount. |
| 43 | Power to remove difficulties | Govt may issue difficulty-removal orders within 3 years of commencement. |
| 44 | Amendments to certain Acts | Amends TRAI Act 1997; **omits §43A of the IT Act 2000**; amends §81 & §87 IT Act; substitutes §8(1)(j) RTI Act 2005. |

## Key definitions from §2 (verbatim / near-verbatim)

- **§2(j) "Data Principal"** — "the individual to whom the personal data relates and where such individual is— (i) a child, includes the parents or lawful guardian of such a child; (ii) a person with disability, includes her lawful guardian, acting on her behalf".
- **§2(i) "Data Fiduciary"** — "any person who alone or in conjunction with other persons determines the purpose and means of processing of personal data".
- **§2(k) "Data Processor"** — "any person who processes personal data on behalf of a Data Fiduciary".
- **§2(t) "personal data"** — "any data about an individual who is identifiable by or in relation to such data".
- **§2(x) "processing"** — "in relation to personal data, means a wholly or partly automated operation or set of operations performed on digital personal data, and includes operations such as collection, recording, organisation, structuring, storage, adaptation, retrieval, use, alignment or combination, indexing, sharing, disclosure by transmission, dissemination or otherwise making available, restriction, erasure or destruction".
- **§6(1) "consent"** — consent "shall be free, specific, informed, unconditional and unambiguous with a clear affirmative action, and shall signify an agreement to the processing of her personal data for the specified purpose and be limited to such personal data as is necessary for such specified purpose".
- **§2(g) "Consent Manager"** — "a person registered with the Board, who acts as a single point of contact to enable a Data Principal to give, manage, review and withdraw her consent through an accessible, transparent and interoperable platform".
- **§2(z) "Significant Data Fiduciary"** — "any Data Fiduciary or class of Data Fiduciaries as may be notified by the Central Government under section 10".
- **§2(c) "Board"** — "the Data Protection Board of India established by the Central Government under section 18".
- **§2(f) "child"** — "an individual who has not completed the age of eighteen years".
- **§2(u) "personal data breach"** — "any unauthorised processing of personal data or accidental disclosure, acquisition, sharing, use, alteration, destruction or loss of access to personal data, that compromises the confidentiality, integrity or availability of personal data".
- **§2(n) "digital personal data"** — "personal data in digital form".

## Most audit-relevant sections (for engineers auditing an app/codebase)

| Area | Sections | Audit focus |
|---|---|---|
| Lawful basis | §4, §7 | Every processing activity maps to consent or a listed legitimate use. |
| Notice & consent flow | §5, §6 | Notice contents, plain language, itemised consent, easy withdrawal, Consent Manager support. |
| Core fiduciary duties | §8 | Processor contracts, data accuracy, **security safeguards**, breach intimation, erasure/retention, DPO contact, grievance mechanism. |
| Children | §9 | Verifiable parental consent; **no tracking / behavioural monitoring / targeted ads** to under-18s. |
| SDF duties | §10 | India-based DPO, independent data audit, periodic DPIA. |
| Data Principal rights | §11–§14 | Access, correction/erasure, grievance redressal, nomination — must be technically supported. |
| Cross-border transfer | §16 | Honour notified-country transfer restrictions (blacklist). |
| Penalties | §33 + Schedule | Map gaps to the breach categories and their monetary caps. |
